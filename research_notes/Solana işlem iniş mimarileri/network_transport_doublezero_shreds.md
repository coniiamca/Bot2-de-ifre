# Solana Network & Transport Layer (as of 9 Oct 2026): DoubleZero, Turbine/shreds, shred feeds, leader geography, kernel bypass

Scope note: this covers the network and transport layer only, meaning how packets physically and logically reach leaders and how block data (shreds) reaches everyone else. Validator scheduling, priority fees and SWQoS, Jito bundles/BAM/Harmonic/Paladin, commercial sending APIs, and the consensus roadmap (Alpenglow/MCP/ACE) are mentioned only where they change transport. Labels used below: **[DEPLOYED]** = live on mainnet; **[PROPOSAL]** = SIMD or PR not confirmed active; **[HISTORICAL]** = superseded but dated; **[VENDOR CLAIM]** = number from a party that sells the product.

Context that changes every latency budget in this note: slot time is being cut under SIMD-0525, from 400ms to 350, 300 and 250ms "since August" 2026. The 200ms gate was pending on mainnet, with 200ms slots expected at epoch 1053, around 15:00 UTC on 9 Oct 2026 (that is, today), per Anza's schedule. Solana Compass had not verified the on-chain state — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solanas-200ms-slot-time-is-pending-on-mainnet-scheduled-to-go-live-at-epoch-1053-on-october-9). The August Solana changelog listed only 400→350ms on mainnet at that time, so the later steps came after August — [Solana Changelog Aug 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-august-20-2026); the Aug 27 changelog lists a mainnet gate "to reduce slot time to 300ms" — [Solana Changelog Aug 27 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-august-27-2026); the Sept 18 changelog lists "Slot time reduction to 250ms" — [Solana Changelog Sep 18 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-september-18-2026).

## 1. DoubleZero: what it is, who built it, architecture, launch, 2Z, adoption, measured impact, how senders use it, criticisms

### Takeaway
DoubleZero (DZ) is a dedicated-fiber overlay network for validators and trading infrastructure. Malbec Labs built it, and Austin Federa and Andrew McConnell co-founded the project. Mainnet-beta went live on 2 Oct 2025 with about 22% of stake. By Q2 2026 it carried 59% of stake (462 validators, 166 links, 10.14 Tbps), and its shred network covered about 62.6% of stake by Aug/Sep 2026. Its most trader-relevant product is **DoubleZero Edge** (launched 16 Apr 2026), a paid multicast feed of raw leader shreds. Edge became the de facto replacement for Jito ShredStream, which shut down on 5 Sep 2026. The FPGA inbound spam/dedup filtering in the original whitepaper is, per DZ's own docs, **not yet functional**. Measured gains are small in Frankfurt (single-digit ms at the tail) and larger, per DZ's own claims, far from Europe and under congestion.

**Architecture [DEPLOYED except where noted]**
- Contributors supply "underutilized private fiber links and hardware (e.g. switches and FPGA devices)" in exchange for token incentives. The network "is compatible with blockchain validator clients as well as RPC and MEV infrastructure providers", and admission requires verifying identifying public keys on the respective ledger — [DoubleZero docs (docs.malbeclabs.com)](https://docs.malbeclabs.com/)
- **Edge filtering status:** the docs say inbound transactions *can* be filtered at contributor hardware to remove spam and duplicates, and also state: "This feature is not yet functional." — [DoubleZero docs](https://docs.malbeclabs.com/). The design is two rings: an outer ring of FPGAs at ingress (filter, DDoS mitigation, signature verification, dedup) and an inner ring of dedicated fiber mesh for transport. This is described in secondary whitepaper summaries — [4Pillars whitepaper summary](https://4pillars.io/en/reports/doublezero-whitepaper); [Figment first look](https://www.figment.io/insights/doublezero-first-look-the-internet-layer-for-solana-and-beyond/)
- The docs navigation lists "Solana IBRL (connect validator; permissioned/RPC)", "Solana Edge (publish and subscribe to shreds, validator rewards)", and other ecosystems (Hyperliquid, Binance, Kalshi, Phoenix, Shelby) — [DoubleZero docs](https://docs.malbeclabs.com/)
- Links meet at DoubleZero Exchange (DZX) points. The network is "smart-contract-defined": link info, SLA data and routing requests sit in on-chain contracts. Validators opt in "with fallback" to their normal internet. The focus is validator-to-validator traffic (lower variance, less packet loss, better shred propagation). Contributors named include Jump Crypto, Galaxy, RockawayX and Cumberland, with "150+" links (Kiln, 16 Feb 2026) — [Kiln](https://www.kiln.fi/post/doublezero-on-solana-a-dive-into-the-internet-layer-for-validators)
- Jump Crypto, Galaxy, RockawayX and Jito were named as contributing fiber links and engineering resources at launch — [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2025/10/01/doublezero-mainnet-goes-live-with-nearly-21-of-staked-sol-on-board)
- Fiber comes from 14 independent operators across 30+ metro areas. Any staked validator, verified through gossip, can connect for free and publish shreds, and anyone can subscribe and pay on-chain in USDC. An earlier "write-side" product sped up *inbound transaction delivery* to connected validators but left shred propagation unchanged (Triton/DZ co-authored post, updated 20 May 2026) — [Triton blog](https://blog.triton.one/rethinking-the-network-beneath-the-blockchain/)
- **Multicast:** the leader transmits once, and switching hardware replicates at the last hop nearest each receiver, so delivery time depends on physical distance from the leader rather than on stake-ordered tree position — [Triton blog](https://blog.triton.one/rethinking-the-network-beneath-the-blockchain/). Transport configuration, multicast group definitions and permissioning are programmable on-chain via the DZ ledger — [RockawayX](https://rockawayx.com/insights/doublezero-launches-native-multicast-for-decentralized-systems). Malbec Labs argues multicast "does not exist and cannot exist on the public internet" — [Breakpoint 25 talk summary](https://solanacompass.com/learn/breakpoint-25/tech-talk-malbec-labs)
- Agave 4.0 notes: "XDP now also works over DoubleZero" — [Anza Agave 4.0 patch notes](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/agave-4.0-patch-notes)

### Inferences
- With ~60% of stake on DZ and Jito ShredStream gone, DZ Edge multicast is now the dominant path for "earliest leader shreds". A bot not on Edge, or on an RPC that does not license it, is likely structurally behind at the tail (p95/p99) even if p50 looks similar.
- The Frankfurt numbers (≈1–15ms) are small because most leaders already sit in Frankfurt/Amsterdam, close to the receiver. DZ's own congestion claims (+80ms US, +100ms Asia) suggest the benefit grows with distance from the leader and under load. This matters most for bots located outside the FRA/AMS hub or reacting to non-European leaders.
- On the send side, DZ benefits come from connected validators/RPCs forwarding to connected leaders over fiber. The marketed spam/dedup FPGA filter is not active, so DZ does not currently protect leader TPU ingress from spam.
- A 5% cut of validator block rewards plus 2Z/USDC flows creates economic lock-in. Network topology is increasingly shaped by a single (on-chain-coordinated but company-led) operator.

## 2. Turbine and shred propagation today (fanout, erasure coding, 2025–2026 changes; Rotor briefly)

### Takeaway
Turbine is still the default propagation protocol [DEPLOYED]. The leader splits a block into ~1.2KB Merkle shreds in **fixed 32 data + 32 coding FEC sets** (Reed-Solomon) and sends each shred to a per-shred, stake-weighted, deterministically shuffled tree with **fanout 200**, so most nodes are 2–3 hops away. The 2025–2026 changes are hardening rather than redesign: chained Merkle shreds only (SIMD-0313), mandatory 32:32 (SIMD-0317), stricter validation (SIMD-0504, in review), and XDP for retransmit (section 5). Alpenglow's Rotor would replace the multi-hop tree with a single relay layer that natively supports multicast systems like DZ, but it is unscheduled.

### Cited Findings
- Fanout `DATA_PLANE_FANOUT` = 200, tree typically 2–3 hops (leader → root → L1 → L2), so most validators are "only 2-3 hops away". The root sorts validators by stake (higher stake gets shreds sooner), then shuffles deterministically with a seed from slot leader ID, slot, shred index and shred type. A new tree is generated for each shred group. Erasure coding is "typically 32:32" Reed-Solomon (up to 32 of 64 packets can be lost; ~99% block success at the article's 15% loss assumption). Missing data falls back to repair — [Helius, Turbine](https://www.helius.dev/blog/turbine-block-propagation-on-solana)
- Large validators "can approach 150,000 outbound packets per second" in retransmit; Turbine fanout is about 200 nodes per relay — [Anza XDP setup guide (23 Sep 2025)](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/agave-xdp-setup-guide)
- Shred packets are about 1200 bytes on the wire (Jito troubleshooting guidance) — [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnfeed/)
- Turbine's structural latency problem: stake ordering means a high-stake distant validator can receive shreds before a low-stake validator in the leader's own data center. Routes can zigzag (example: a Singapore shred via London and LA back to Singapore), and each hop adds latency — [Triton blog](https://blog.triton.one/rethinking-the-network-beneath-the-blockchain/)
- **SIMD-0317 (enforce exactly 32 data + 32 coding shreds per FEC set)**: created Jul 2025. It notes clients already send 32+32 but the protocol allowed variable counts, and fixed sets simplify validation and equivocation detection — [SIMD-0317 text](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/blob/main/proposals/0317-enforce-32-data-shreds.md); [Anza thread, Jan 2026](https://x.com/anza_xyz/status/2008548399939989717)
- **Activation status (unverified):** PR #653 (opened 22 Sep 2026, closed unmerged 25 Sep 2026) claims SIMD-0313 `drop_unchained_merkle_shreds` activated at slot 354,672,000 and SIMD-0317 `enforce_fixed_fec_set` (ID `fixfecLZYMfkGzwq6NJA11Yw6KYztzXiK9QcL3K78in`) at slot 403,920,000. Payload math: 963 bytes/data shred with the chained 32-byte Merkle root (30,816 bytes/FEC set), and **899 bytes/shred (28,768/FEC set) when "resigned" with a 64-byte retransmitter signature** — [PR #653](https://github.com/solana-foundation/solana-improvement-documents/pull/653); the SIMD-0317 doc still showed "Review" status in the snapshot seen.
- **SIMD-0504 (stricter shred validation)** [PROPOSAL, status Review, 13 Mar 2026, Philip Taffet/Firedancer]: all shreds in a FEC set must share signature, chained Merkle root, type, slot, shred version and fec_set_index; Merkle height 6; requires 32/32. Under TowerBFT a validator MUST NOT vote for a block with a failing FEC set; under Alpenglow it must vote skip. Checks also apply to recovered shreds — [simd.live SIMD-0504](https://simd.live/simd/0504-stricter-shred-validation); Agave tracking issue — [agave #11790](https://github.com/anza-xyz/agave/issues/11790)
- **Rotor (Alpenglow) [PROPOSAL/unscheduled]**: Rotor "follows Votor to replace Turbine's tree-based block propagation with a single relay layer", marked "Later release · Unscheduled" — [solana.com/alpenglow](https://solana.com/alpenglow). Under Rotor, relay nodes distribute shreds in a one-hop model and each shred is a single erasure-coded packet; the design "natively supports multicast systems such as DoubleZero" — [Helius Alpenglow](https://www.helius.dev/es/blog/alpenglow). Agave 4.2 ships Alpenglow feature-complete, with activation "now expected with Agave 4.3" — [Helius Agave 4.2](https://www.helius.dev/blog/agave-v4-2). Firedancer is building Rotor FECs and Rotor repair responses — [Solana Changelog Aug 27 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-august-27-2026)
- 100M CU blocks went live on mainnet on 29 Jul 2026 (up 66% from 60M), which increases shreds per block — [solana.com XDP page](https://solana.com/upgrades/xdp)

### Inferences
- For a bot, Turbine position is a function of *your node's stake*, not your geography. A non-staked RPC/bot node is at the bottom of the tree (last hop), which is why paid shred feeds (direct from leader or via multicast) exist.
- Fixed 32:32 sets mean a receiver can start deshredding entries once any 32 of 64 shreds in a set arrive. Shred-feed consumers therefore see transactions FEC set by FEC set during the slot, not at slot end.
- 100M CU blocks plus 200ms slots raise shred rate per second (roughly 2× from slot time alone, plus bigger blocks). Packet-processing efficiency (XDP) and multicast become more important, and the cost of extra hops grows relative to the slot.

### Gaps
- No primary on-chain confirmation found for SIMD-0317/0313 activation slots (PR #653 is closed and unreviewed).
- No 2026 measurement of end-to-end Turbine propagation time distribution (e.g., p50/p99 time from leader to 2/3 stake) was found.

## 3. Shred feeds: Jito ShredStream, DZ Edge, bloXroute and others; Geyser vs shreds; latency numbers

### Takeaway
Shred feeds give the earliest state signal: transactions as the leader emits them, before execution and with no logs or status. Geyser/Yellowstone gRPC arrives after replay with full metadata. **Jito ShredStream was shut down on 5 Sep 2026**, and Jito pointed users to DoubleZero Edge. The remaining market is DZ Edge (raw multicast UDP, licensed), resellers that race multiple sources (Triton, Shyft RabbitStream, Shreder, OrbitFlare, etc.) and bloXroute OFR. Published gaps between feeds are a few ms at p50 and tens of ms at p99. Shred feeds lead Geyser by tens to low hundreds of ms. Almost all numbers are vendor-run.

### Cited Findings
**Jito ShredStream [HISTORICAL as of 5 Sep 2026]**
- Architecture: a proxy client authenticates with a keypair (pre-approved via Discord) to the Jito Block Engine, sends heartbeats, receives unicast shreds on UDP 20000 (no NAT) and forwards them to `DEST_IP_PORTS`; up to 2 `DESIRED_REGIONS`; regions included Amsterdam, Dublin, Frankfurt, London, New York, Salt Lake City, Singapore and Tokyo. An optional gRPC server streams deshredded `Entry` objects. **Deprecation:** "completely shut down in 60 days (September 5, 2026)", with migration to DoubleZero Edge (free trial, guide at doublezero.xyz/jito-shredstream) — [Jito docs](https://docs.jito.wtf/lowlatencytxnfeed/)
- Jito ShredStream ceased operations on 5 Sep 2026 — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/doublezero-removes-unauthorized-shred-retransmitters-claims-70-plus-lead-over-all-competitors); vendor migration guide — [OrbitFlare (dev.to)](https://dev.to/orbitflarerpc/jito-shredstreams-last-slot-your-two-ways-out-53np)
- Even before shutdown, Jito's own distribution used DZ multicast to reach ShredStream proxies worldwide (Breakpoint 2025 case study) — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/learn/breakpoint-25/tech-talk-malbec-labs)

**Benchmarks between shred feeds [VENDOR CLAIMS]**
- Shyft (21 May 2026, geyserbench v1.2.2, ~10k tx/run, 2 runs/region): RabbitStream arrived first 96.5–97.8% of the time. Jito lagged by **p50 6.72/4.19ms and p99 46.86/25.74ms (Frankfurt)** and **p50 5.29/3.87ms and p99 81.5/82.06ms (New York)**. Both received 100% of valid tx. RabbitStream ingests raw shreds from multiple sources and forwards the first arrival via Yellowstone-compatible filters — [Shyft](https://shyft.to/blog/rabbitstream-vs-jito-shredstream)

- bloXroute Optimized Feed Relay (OFR): claims "30-50+ ms" faster shreds than default Turbine propagation. "Premium regions" come from partnerships with top staked validators representing ">10% of total staked SOL" — [bloXroute OFR docs](https://docs.bloxroute.com/solana/optimized-feed-relay); [bloXroute premium regions](https://docs.bloxroute.com/solana/optimized-feed-relay/shred-stream-premium-regions). - Triton races four sources (DZ Edge, Jito ShredStream, Turbine, Triton's own leaders) and fans out via internal multicast to every RPC in the PoP. Jito is now gone from that set — [Triton blog](https://blog.triton.one/rethinking-the-network-beneath-the-blockchain/); Triton receives Edge shreds via a "special partnership" — [Triton shred streaming](https://triton.one/products/triton-shred-streaming)
- Other raw-shred forwarding offerings exist, e.g., Solana Vibe Station — [SVS docs](https://docs.solanavibestation.com/services/raw-shred-forwarding)

**Shreds vs Geyser/Yellowstone gRPC**
- Shred-derived streams see transactions "as the block is produced, before execution metadata exists". Geyser streams arrive after the bank processes the tx, with logs, balance changes, CU usage and status. The shred path increases client-side parsing/correctness work — [RPC Fast, ShredStream vs Geyser](https://rpcfast.com/blog/shredstream-vs-geyser-vs-standard-rpc)
- RPC Fast case: adding Jito ShredStream to its nodes improved Yellowstone gRPC arrival time by **avg 120ms, p99 ~270ms**. The same vendor's docs claim "~2 minutes earlier", which is physically implausible and should be ignored — [RPC Fast docs](https://docs.rpcfast.com/solana/jito-shredstream-grpc); [RPC Fast](https://rpcfast.com/blog/shredstream-vs-geyser-vs-standard-rpc)
- Agave 4.0 brought faster gRPC streams (Triton notes on Agave 4.0) — [Triton Agave 4.0 blog](https://blog.triton.one/agave-4-0-faster-grpc-streams-xdp-and-direct-i-o-snapshots/)

### Inferences
- Bots use shreds because a pending swap or liquidation becomes visible mid-slot, as soon as its FEC set arrives. Geyser only fires after replay of that slot's entries. With 200ms slots, the 100+ms shred-vs-Geyser lead is a large fraction of a slot.
- After Jito's exit, feed diversity is lower. Most third-party "fastest shred" products appear to race DZ Edge against Turbine and their own validators' shreds, so DZ Edge is now a common upstream dependency (single point of commercial and technical dependence).
- Raw shreds give no execution result. Bots acting on shreds must simulate or predict outcomes themselves and handle duplicates/equivocation (the SIMD-0504 rules define what a valid FEC set looks like).

### Gaps
- No neutral, third-party, methodology-published benchmark comparing DZ Edge, bloXroute OFR and reseller feeds post-Sep 2026.
- No confirmed count of how many shred-feed vendors source from DZ Edge under license.
- Triton's May 2026 "90% of slots" analysis was not found in primary form.

## 4. Geography: where stake and leaders sit, and how this affects send strategy

### Takeaway
Leader slots are overwhelmingly European. As of Sep 2026, Europe held ~73% of leader slots and Frankfurt alone ~35%; Frankfurt + Amsterdam produced ~52% of blocks with ~53% of stake. By country (9 Oct 2026): Germany 34.3%, Netherlands 21.3%, US 14%, UK 9.1%, Japan 5.8%. Hosting is concentrated too: TeraSwitch AS20326 has ~28% of stake. On 12 Aug 2026 a TeraSwitch routing failure pushed delinquent stake to a record 28.83%, within ~4.5pp of the 33.3% finality threshold. For sending, a Frankfurt/Amsterdam presence covers most leaders. US/Asia presence matters only for the minority of slots led there, which DZDP Phase II is trying to grow.

### Cited Findings
- validators.solutions snapshot (9 Oct 2026, 678/681 validators geolocated): **countries:** Germany 191 validators / 34.3%, Netherlands 121 / 21.3%, US 113 / 14%, UK 30 / 9.1%, Japan 55 / 5.8%, Lithuania 17 / 4.5%, Singapore 46 / 4.3%, Sweden 1.9%, Ireland 1.3%, Hong Kong 0.7%. **Providers:** TeraSwitch 98 validators / 28.2%, Latitude.sh 106 / 16.1%, Cherry Servers 88 / 13.9%, Allnodes 8.9%, Servers.com 4.8%, Amazon 4.3%, Galaxy 3.1%, Payward (Kraken) 2.9%, OVH 2.3%. **ASNs:** AS20326 TeraSwitch 28.2%, AS396356 Latitude 13.9%, AS395201 Allnodes 8.9%, AS7979 Servers.com 4.8%, Cherry across AS16125/AS213896/AS59642 (4.5/3.5/2.9%), AS14618 Amazon 2.8% — [validators.solutions data centers](https://validators.solutions/en/validators/data-centers/)

AS20326 held ~27.34% (~118.89M SOL). Peak delinquent stake hit 28.83% (a record), ~4.5pp from the 33.34% threshold; ~90 validators (or 102 of 699) affected; 12 sites; delinquency ~33 min; no halt and finality maintained — [Bitcoin Foundation analysis (citing Marinade, SolanaFloor, Solana Compass)](https://bitcoinfoundation.org/news/analysis/solana-outage-2026-what-the-validator-failure-means-for-sols-next-rally/)

- DZDP Phase II (from 9 Mar 2026) directs ~2.4M SOL to São Paulo, Singapore, Hong Kong and Tokyo — [Datawallet](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/doublezero-explained); [BEX](https://bex.co/blog/2026/03/11/doublezero-phase-2-solana-geographic-decentralization)
- Leader-schedule change proposal (SIMD PR #612) aims to keep a validator's leader slots within one 4-slot window [PROPOSAL] — [Solana Changelog Aug 27 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-august-27-2026)
- Agave PR #15204: the TPU client will "preemptively estimate how much time it has left to send transactions to the leader", or send to the next leader instead, as slot times fall — [Solana Changelog Sep 18 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-september-18-2026)

### Inferences
- Send strategy: ~3/4 of leader slots are in Europe and about a third in Frankfurt alone, so a sender colocated in Frankfurt (and Amsterdam) is within single-digit ms of the majority of leaders. Senders in NY or Tokyo pay a transatlantic/transpacific penalty on most slots. The common pattern is multi-region senders that route each tx toward the PoP nearest the *current and next* leader's IP (leader schedule + gossip contact info).
- With 200ms slots and 800ms leader windows, a transatlantic one-way delay is a larger share of a slot. Mis-targeting the leader (sending to one whose window has ended) costs more, which is why Agave added time-left estimation.
- ASN concentration is a real network risk for bots. A single provider's BGP mistake (Aug 2026) can make ~28% of stake, and hence a proportional share of upcoming leaders, unreachable for minutes. Bots should treat leader reachability as probabilistic and fall back to the next leaders.
- Pressure from the SFDP <25% ASN rule and DZDP APAC incentives may slowly spread leaders out. As of Oct 2026, TeraSwitch (28.2%) still exceeds the 25% rule.

### Gaps
- No authoritative city-level leader-slot share for Amsterdam, London, NY or Tokyo was found for Oct 2026. The Crypto Briefing leader-slot "snapshot" source is unnamed.
- validators.solutions' Frankfurt city page (May 2026) showed only 10 validators/3.6%, conflicting with the 110/28.3% figure, likely due to city-attribution method.
- No published inter-city RTT matrix for Solana leaders (e.g., FRA↔NY, FRA↔TYO) from a primary measurement source.

## 5. Kernel bypass and NIC-level work: Firedancer AF_XDP, Agave XDP

### Takeaway
Kernel bypass is now mainstream. Firedancer has used AF_XDP for all its networking since inception. Agave added XDP for Turbine retransmit in late 2025: experimental flags in 2.3.8/3.0.9+, opt-in in 4.0 (May 2026), non-experimental in 4.1, and XDP transmit **on by default in 4.2** (zero-copy still manual). By mid-2026 >2/3 (later >70%) of stake ran XDP. Anza reports retransmit time on large validators falling from **~600ms to ~0.8ms**. XDP is being extended to TPU QUIC egress and Alpenglow vote egress. This raises shred egress capacity (a prerequisite for 100M CU blocks) but mainly benefits *block propagation*, not inbound tx ingest, on Agave.

### Cited Findings
**Agave**
- [HISTORICAL] Sep 2025: "Agave v2.3.8+ adds XDP to accelerate Turbine block propagation… prepares validators for 100M CU blocks" — [Anza on X](https://x.com/anza_xyz/status/1970506077017121262). Setup guide (23 Sep 2025): flags `--experimental-retransmit-xdp-cpu-cores`, `--experimental-retransmit-xdp-zero-copy` (not with bnxt_en or ice drivers), `--experimental-retransmit-xdp-interface` (bond member); XDP and PoH on separate physical cores; requires Agave v3.0.9+; kernel ≥6.8 recommended (igb zero-copy needs 6.14); large validators approach 150k outbound pps — [Anza XDP setup guide](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/agave-xdp-setup-guide)
- Agave 4.0 (patch notes dated 19 May 2026, mainnet rollout targeted May 2026): XDP loads an eBPF program near the NIC so shred traffic skips most of the Linux stack. **Turbine retransmit dropped "from 600ms to ~0.8ms"** on large mainnet validators. XDP works over DoubleZero. Capability hardening (CAP_NET_RAW, CAP_NET_ADMIN, CAP_BPF, CAP_PERFMON) — [Anza Agave 4.0 patch notes](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/agave-4.0-patch-notes)
- solana.com (updated Aug 2026): supported from 4.0.0 (opt-in), experimental label removed in 4.1, default in 4.2; zero-copy not default; "more than two-thirds of network stake has enabled XDP" and later ">70% of mainnet stake runs XDP"; Firedancer has used XDP by default since inception; the page claims XDP "reduces latency by up to 200x" — [solana.com/upgrades/xdp](https://solana.com/upgrades/xdp)
- A supermajority of stake had enabled XDP by 30 Jun 2026 (Anza's Tim Garcia) — [Solana Compass, Agave 4.2 schedule](https://solanacompass.com/news/anza-sets-august-target-for-200ms-slots-larger-transactions-and-rent-reduction-as-xdp-clears-supermajority)
- Agave 4.2: "XDP transmit is now enabled by default" — [Helius Agave 4.2](https://www.helius.dev/blog/agave-v4-2)
- Triton describes Agave XDP as an eBPF program near the NIC handing shreds to the validator via a shared-memory ring — [Triton Agave 4.0 blog](https://blog.triton.one/agave-4-0-faster-grpc-streams-xdp-and-direct-i-o-snapshots/)
- [IN PROGRESS] Agave PR #10820 "streamer: add quic af_xdp for egress" adds QUIC transmit over AF_XDP, optionally for TPU transactions (excluding votes) — [agave PR #10820](https://github.com/anza-xyz/agave/pull/10820)
- [IN PROGRESS] Agave PR #15347: Votor (Alpenglow) vote egress via XDP, changing "the protocol by which votes are sent from QUIC datagrams to XDP over QUIC" — [Solana Changelog Sep 18 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-september-18-2026)

**Firedancer**
- The net tile uses AF_XDP (drv mode is the fast path; skb fallback), zero-copy via UMEM (2048-byte frames), and busy polling (wakes the kernel ~20k times/s for batched RX/TX). Each net tile owns one dedicated core and one NIC queue. The **target is ~20M packets/s RX**, achieved in a PoC (Ivy Bridge + Intel XL710, i40e drv mode, zero copy), though the current net tile "achieves far less". QUIC and shred tiles are RX consumers. GRO/UDP segmentation offloads must be disabled. One external interface only. No IPv6. Cannot share the interface with other AF_XDP apps — [Firedancer docs, net tile](https://docs.firedancer.io/guide/internals/net_tile.html)
- [HISTORICAL, 2023] fd_quic: a Firedancer QUIC server on 4 cores ingested 21.8 Gbps of transactions — [Jump Firedancer on X](https://x.com/jump_firedancer/status/1654124396062158850)
- Firedancer replaced OpenSSL with its own TLS implementation (PR #11452) and built an X.509 verifier, affecting the QUIC handshake path — [Solana Changelog Sep 18 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-september-18-2026); [Solana Changelog Aug 27 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-august-27-2026)
- Firedancer-family stake share estimates conflict (~14–26% for Frankendancer + Firedancer; full Firedancer ~11–14%), and Frankendancer support is reported to end at Alpenglow activation — [CryptoTicker](https://cryptoticker.io/en/solana-price-prediction-frankendancer-shutdown-validator-check/); [BEX](https://bex.co/blog/2026/05/02/firedancer-v1-1m-immunefi-audit-solana-multi-client-diversity)

### Inferences
- The ~600ms→0.8ms retransmit improvement means that, on XDP-enabled relays, per-hop processing delay inside the validator is now negligible relative to fiber distance. Turbine latency is now dominated by geography and tree depth, which is exactly what multicast/DZ attacks.
- For tx senders, kernel bypass on the *leader's ingest* path matters mostly under spam floods. Firedancer leaders have had AF_XDP ingest since inception, while Agave's XDP so far covers egress (retransmit, TPU-client egress, votes). Leader client type may therefore influence how well a leader absorbs bursts. This is inference; no measurement was found.
- Operator-side caveats (driver support, zero-copy bugs with bnxt_en/ice, cores dedicated to XDP) mean XDP benefits are uneven across validators.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of Agave inbound TPU ingest capacity with vs without XDP, and no current measured Firedancer net-tile pps on mainnet hardware.
- No independent verification of the "up to 200x" claim.

## 6. Other experimental or new networking changes (QUIC, multicast for TPU, SIMDs affecting transport)

### Takeaway
No proposal to *replace* QUIC for transaction ingestion was found. Instead, Agave 4.0 (May 2026) made QUIC the **only** TPU path, removing UDP. The client side is consolidating on `tpu-client-next` over QUIC streams, and v1 transactions up to 4,096 bytes (SIMD-0296/0385) can span multiple packets because QUIC streams lift the 1,232-byte single-datagram cap. Transport-adjacent work in flight: XDP for QUIC egress and votes, Firedancer's own TLS, leader-window-aware sending, slot time cuts (SIMD-0525), stricter shred validation (SIMD-0504) and Rotor (multicast-friendly). No SIMD for multicast TPU ingress was found.

### Cited Findings
- **QUIC-only TPU [DEPLOYED in Agave 4.0]:** UDP transaction ingestion removed; QUIC is the only way to submit. `--public-tpu-address`/`--public-tpu-forwards-address` now specify QUIC ports. Flags `--use-quic`, `--use-udp`, `--tpu-disable-quic` and `--tpu-enable-udp` removed. `getClusterNodes` adds a `clientId` field — [Anza Agave 4.0 patch notes](https://www.anza.xyz/blog/agave-4.0-patch-notes)
- Agave "hardened its TPU client API to use QUIC streams rather than QUIC datagrams" (PR #14810) and plans to deprecate the original TPU client in favor of `tpu-client-next` (PR #14872) — [Solana Changelog Aug 27 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-august-27-2026)
- **Transaction v1 (SIMD-0296 size, SIMD-0385 format):** raises the max size from 1,232 bytes (from the 1,280-byte IPv6 minimum MTU minus 40-byte IPv6 and 8-byte UDP headers) to **4,096 bytes**, possible because QUIC streams aren't limited to one datagram. Legacy/v0 stay at 1,232 — [Helius Agave 4.2](https://www.helius.dev/blog/agave-v4-2). Mainnet targeted 9 Sep 2026 per Solana Compass — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solana-transaction-v1-heads-to-mainnet-september-9-format-breaking-changes-and-what-it-unlocks); the "Transaction V1" mainnet feature gate is listed in the Sep 18 changelog — [Solana Changelog Sep 18 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-september-18-2026)
- XDP for TPU QUIC egress (PR #10820) and Votor vote egress (PR #15347) — see section 5 — [agave PR #10820](https://github.com/anza-xyz/agave/pull/10820); [Solana Changelog Sep 18 2026](https://solana.com/news/solana-changelog-september-18-2026)
- SIMD-0504 mentions SIMD-0376 ("verify-strict" signature verification), under which a crafted key could produce multiple valid signatures for the same Merkle root — [simd.live SIMD-0504](https://simd.live/simd/0504-stricter-shred-validation)
- Slot-time reduction SIMD-0525 (400→350→300→250→200ms; each gate takes effect with a one-epoch delay) — [Solana Compass](https://solanacompass.com/news/solanas-200ms-slot-time-is-pending-on-mainnet-scheduled-to-go-live-at-epoch-1053-on-october-9)
- Rotor's single relay layer is designed to support multicast natively (DZ) [PROPOSAL, unscheduled] — [Helius Alpenglow](https://www.helius.dev/es/blog/alpenglow); [solana.com/alpenglow](https://solana.com/alpenglow)
- DZ Edge is positioned to carry other feeds (block-engine pre-confirmation signals, equities, commodities) — [Triton blog](https://blog.triton.one/rethinking-the-network-beneath-the-blockchain/)

### Inferences
- For senders, the transport is settled on QUIC streams to the leader's TPU (or forwards) port, with connection setup (TLS handshake) and per-connection stream limits (SWQoS, covered elsewhere) as the main transport-level frictions. Pre-warmed QUIC connections to upcoming leaders matter more as slots shrink.
- Multi-packet v1 transactions over QUIC streams add a small amount of serialization/loss sensitivity compared with single-packet transactions. Latency-critical bots may keep transactions ≤1,232 bytes where possible (inference, not measured).
- The architectural direction (Rotor + DZ multicast + XDP everywhere) moves block data toward one-hop, physically optimal delivery. On the ingress side, no comparable "multicast TPU" exists. Inbound remains point-to-point QUIC to the leader, where proximity (Frankfurt/Amsterdam) and leader targeting dominate.

### Gaps
- No SIMD or Anza/Firedancer proposal found that replaces QUIC for TPU or uses multicast for transaction ingress.
- Activation status of Transaction v1 on mainnet (9 Sep 2026 target) and the exact current slot time were not confirmed on-chain in this research.
- No published data on how QUIC handshake/connection limits interact with 200ms slots in practice.
